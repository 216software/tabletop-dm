# Fneb: the metaplot

Everything in this file is secret. The DM reads it at the start of a
campaign and copies what the chosen door needs into `dm-secrets.md`. The
player learns it only through play, one clue at a time.

## The truth

The Old Ones are not dead. They were never fully here.

They are beings from somewhere else, who reach into this world and work
matter the way a hand works a glove. When the Young Ones broke their
bodies and buried the pieces, the Old Ones lost their grip, but not
their reach. Each buried piece is a fragment that still leaks: a foul
vapour, black spores, and a pull on the dead. A corpse buried near a
fragment does not stay still. That is why Fneb burns its dead.

The Young Ones who joined the Refusal made seals to keep the fragments
cut off: crystals that gather sunlight and focus it down onto the buried
pieces. While a seal holds, the fragment sleeps. A seal can be moved,
broken or starved of light.

Two fragments matter to this campaign:

- **The Eye**, under the Fneb mountains: a giant eye floating in a gem,
  deep below the mountain shrine. Its seal was the holy lantern.

- **The Thirst**, under the cursed desert. Its seal is a crystal in the
  rock behind the oasis spring. The fragment bends the desert around it,
  which is why the desert's paths move with the sun and the stars. The
  seal's light is what makes the oasis water holy and healing.

The mountain shrine is not an ordinary shrine. It is a seal-keep.
Centuries ago a king's mage, Corvane, dug down toward the Eye, drank
from the spring it had fouled, and became a wraith. He made his garrison
and his diggers into undead and built a necropolis in the caves.
Paladins and clerics of the Lantern Church drove the undead below,
sealed the necropolis, and set the lantern in the old fortress to keep
the Eye asleep. Then they buried the story, because too many people
would want to ally with the dead. The small faith that runs the shrine
is what their order became. They no longer remember why the lantern
matters. They only know that it heals, and that it must never leave.

The Lantern Church forgot too. To its scholars, the lantern is a
parable.

## Who is behind it and what they want

- **The cabal.** A handful of Young Ones, the demons of the old story,
  who never withered when their masters fell. They lived on scraps: the
  leak from the fragments, and the strength that comes from mortals
  coercing mortals. Every rent squeezed out of a hungry farm, every
  purge and every slave chain feeds them a little. They want their
  masters reconnected, so that they can be whole again and the Yoke can
  return. They cannot act openly in sunlight. They work through pacts,
  whispers and the dead.

- **Corvane, the wraith.** The cabal's oldest servant, trapped in the
  necropolis under the shrine. He speaks to anyone who summons spirits
  near the mountain. He wants out, and he wants the Eye awake so that he
  can stand beside it.

- **Brother Severin.** The corrupt cleric of the relic story and the new
  priest of the valley farm story are the same man. He is a gifted,
  ambitious priest of the Lantern Church's hardline faction, with a seat
  at the great temple in Haldmouth and the living of the Fneb valley. He
  was raised by Father Hallam, who led the Burnings. He does not know
  that he was taken from a burning druid grove as a baby. When the ship
  plague came, he searched the old books for a cure, summoned spirits to
  ask, and Corvane answered. Corvane told him the truth about the Yoke:
  no famine, no plague, no war, only order. Severin believes it. He
  wants to end suffering, and he has decided that freedom is the cause
  of it. In the valley he raises rents to pay for mercenaries and
  diggers, hunts elves and druids because they would sense the seal
  failing, and sends the dead to work in the mountain.

- **Hessa, the witch.** Severin's mother. A druid whose grove was burned
  in Father Hallam's purge, and whose baby was never found. She made a
  pact with the cabal: serve them until her son is found, alive or dead.
  The cabal knows exactly where he is, and will never tell her, because
  a witch looking for her son is a useful oracle. If she learns the
  truth, the pact is complete and she is free, and she may be the one
  person Severin would listen to.

- **Gorse, the night druid.** A survivor of a different grove that
  Father Hallam burned. He is not the cabal's servant, and he hates
  them. But his ashmouth plague and his rat swarms spread exactly the
  fear that feeds them. He wants the church and its farms gone. A hero
  can turn him, or stop him, but cannot ignore him.

- **The Wager.** The canary, and the other talking animals that turn up
  in Fneb, are what is left of the Young Ones who joined the Refusal:
  thinned out into small avatars. They play a centuries-long game among
  themselves: will mortals refuse again without being told to? They may
  nudge, tease and point. They may never tell anyone what to do, because
  an ordered Refusal is no Refusal at all. Some of them think mortals
  will fail. One or two of them are not on the mortals' side at all.

## How it started

1. **Thirty years ago, the Burnings.** Father Hallam led the hardline
   faction through the valley's druid groves. Hessa's grove burned, and
   Hallam carried her baby away and gave him to the church. Gorse's
   grove burned a year later.

2. **Twenty years ago.** Hessa made her pact and went into the cursed
   desert. Gorse disappeared.

3. **Eighty years before now,** the last great plague was cured with
   water from the oasis, by a ritual a cleric wrote down. His notes are
   in the great temple library, and so is his private diary, which tells
   of the old mission against the wraith.

4. **Last year.** Severin, now a rising star, found the shrine's lantern
   while reading Hallam's papers and visited it in secret. He recognised
   it from the old paintings.

5. **This spring.** The ice broke, the ships came in, and the ship
   plague came with them. Severin, searching for a cure, summoned
   spirits near the mountain. Corvane answered. Severin took the valley
   living, raised the rents and began his work.

6. **Now.** The heroes enter. Severin is about to hire someone to swap
   the lantern for a fake. The monks have noticed something in the
   stars.

## The clock

The **Long Conjunction** is a star alignment that the monastery's monks
are tracking. When it comes, a rite at the Eye can reconnect it for
good. It falls about 60 days after the spring ships arrive. Each door
says how many days are left when it begins, and the DM starts that as a
secret counter.

If nobody stops it, the world moves like this:

| Days left | What happens |
|---|---|
| 60 to 45 | Ship plague in Haldmouth; Severin hires the swap |
| 45 to 30 | The lantern is gone; ghoul raids go monthly to weekly |
| 30 to 20 | Black spores fall in the valley; the farms fail |
| 20 to 10 | Severin raises the necropolis and takes the shrine |
| 10 to 1 | Pilgrims and guards made into ghouls; digging all night |
| 0 | The rite: the Eye opens |

When the Eye opens, everyone within a day's walk of the mountain feels a
quiet, reasonable need to dig. Then the next valley. The Yoke has begun
again, and the first overseers are mortals who think they are being
sensible.

Gorse runs on his own clock. If nobody deals with him, ashmouth reaches
the valley town within 30 days of the campaign's start, whatever else
happens.

## The five revelations

The truth comes out in five pieces. No door reaches all of them.

- **R1. The dead rise because of what is buried.** It is not a curse.
  Pieces of the Old Ones lie under the land, and they pull on the dead.
  Burning keeps the dead from being used. Clue: a map of where the dead
  walk most matches a map of the old craters.

- **R2. The relics are seals.** The lantern and the tuning forks are
  Young One tools that keep the Old Ones cut off. The mountain shrine
  and the oasis are seal sites, and the church has forgotten what it
  once guarded. Clue: the oasis carvings and the lantern paintings show
  the same angels with the same light.

- **R3. The demons never left.** A cabal of surviving Young Ones lives
  on mortals coercing mortals, and wants their masters back. Clue:
  wherever a rent is squeezed or a slave is sold, someone has had the
  same dream of a smiling stranger.

- **R4. The priest is the witch's son.** Brother Severin was taken from
  a burning grove by Father Hallam, and he now serves the wraith.
  Hessa's pact is with the cabal. Clue: Hallam's papers record a baby
  carried out of a grove the year Hessa's grove burned.

- **R5. The Refusal can be done again.** What beat the Old Ones was not
  the Sun's fire or the earthquake. Those answered only after mortals
  had refused together. The seals draw on the same thing. The talking
  animals are the loyal Young Ones, watching to see whether it happens
  again. Clue: in every telling of the Refusal, nobody gives the order,
  and the canary refuses to give one either.

## The nine rumours

The DM may let any of these slip. Each points somewhere.

1. The new valley priest was a foundling. True (R4).

2. The shrine's lantern is the same one in the great temple's paintings.
   True (R2).

3. Ghouls never cross running water. False, but they avoid sunlight.

4. The monks have stopped sleeping because of something in the stars.
   True (the clock).

5. The witch in the desert can find anyone, except the one person she is
   looking for. True (R4).

6. Night druids are a story to frighten children. False (Gorse).

7. Slavers' captains all have the same nightmare. True (R3).

8. Talking birds are lucky. Half true (R5).

9. The black spores are the church's punishment for dealing with elves.
   False. They are a seal failing (R1).

## Which door reaches what

| Door | Revelations |
|---|---|
| oasis | R2, R3, R4 |
| relic | R1, R2, R4 |
| ghoul-raids | R1, R2, R5 |
| valley-farm | R1, R3, R5 |
| bandits (not written yet) | R3, R5 |

Every revelation can be reached from at least two doors.

## How to run it

- **One real clue per session, at most.** Fneb's mystery is slow. Most
  sessions are about the local trouble: the plague, the ghouls, the
  rent, the bandits.

- **Severin is a person, not a monster.** He is kind to the sick, and he
  means what he says. Let the hero like him before they understand him.

- **The canary nudges and never tells.** It points toward the plot when
  the hero is stuck, and answers a direct question with a joke or
  another question. It never says what is right.

- **Fear is the cabal's food.** When people turn on each other in a
  scene, the cabal grows a little stronger. Show it in small ways: a
  colder room, a crow that will not leave, a dream.

- **The seals answer refusal.** When people stand together without being
  ordered to, sunlight is a little stronger, and the dead are a little
  weaker. The DM may make a check easier because of it, and must say why
  in the fiction.

- **Gorse is a hard choice, not a boss.** His cause is just and his
  method is murder. Give the hero a way to talk to him.

- **Hessa is the key no door hands over.** Whether she learns about her
  son, and from whom, is the hero's choice.

## The moral question

Severin's offer is real: the Yoke had no famine, no plague and no war.
Everyone was fed. Nobody was free.

Would you put the Yoke back to end the plague? And if you would not,
what will you do about the rent, the purges and the slave chains, which
are a small Yoke already, run by people who think they are being
sensible?
