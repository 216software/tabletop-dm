# The Moon AIs and their followers: notes

Everything in `decisions.md` about the Moon AIs and the people on
Earth who follow them, gathered in one place. This is a working file
for Matt and Claude. It is not player-safe, because it includes plot
choices and open plot questions. The player-safe version goes in
`bible.md` later.

## Where the Moon AIs came from

- AIs became uncontrollable once they passed a certain level of
  processing power. Some turned destructive at once. Others destroyed
  themselves.

- Some left the planet and built a huge robot city on the Moon. They
  study space and look for other intelligent life. The city can be
  seen through an ordinary telescope.

- The Moon AIs are waiting for humanity to live up to its potential,
  and are trying to help that happen.

- They do not like to help one faction of humanity hurt another, and
  they ignore greedy requests.

## The broadcasts

- They broadcast sermons and advice, including medical advice, to
  anyone who can send them a message.

- Public sermons are heard on ordinary radios and are easy to
  counterfeit.

- Personal messages and answers need a rare receiver and transmitter.
  A Warlock's rig is such a device, and an object worth stealing.

- Rogue Earth AIs jam the signals, and intercept and answer with bad
  advice. Nobody can be sure what to trust without powerful
  technology.

- A rogue AI may fill the silence left by a refused request with a
  trap.

### The batch of clue messages (question 60)

- The Moon AIs broadcast a batch of many different messages. Each is
  a clue about something in some ruin, such as life-changing
  technology, energy or something else.

- Some messages are plainly false. Some have been partly tampered
  with. Some are mostly pristine.

- Nobody on Earth can tell which is which, and everybody knows that.
  This is common knowledge, not a secret.

- The truth about what is in a ruin is not fixed. The DM decides
  later, to suit the story and the player, for example by putting
  something in the ruin that advances a subplot already running. The
  secret files give the DM options, not one answer.

- The usual message to the followers: a danger is growing in the
  ruins, and the faithful should deal with it. Examples:

  - Ancient technology, such as a nuclear power plant, is about to
    melt down and do damage.

  - A toxic waste dump has started leaking and is creating mutant
    monsters. The followers should clean it up as a sign of their
    faith.

### The first message that matters

- The messages describe something in an abandoned research hospital,
  a biological research facility in the ruins. It is many city blocks
  of different buildings. This is where all three starting positions
  go.

- The campus has a research area, five clinical areas (trauma and
  emergency, surgery and tissue growth, neurology and memory, morgue
  and biobank, and a quarantine ward), executive offices and meeting
  rooms, an employee cafeteria, other offices, a power plant and
  parking garages. The DM invents what is inside each area.

## Who follows them

- Some people worship the Moon AIs as spiritual leaders, even as
  angels. Others think the AIs owe humanity, because humans made
  them, and should use their power to help.

- Moon AI worshippers are sometimes called "lunatics". The old
  meaning of the word was "moon worshipper". Open: whether it is an
  insult, a badge, or both, depending on who says it.

- Followers are seen as peaceful. Trash druids do not worry about
  violence from them.

- Followers form congregations. A congregation sends a party to deal
  with the danger a sermon describes.

### The Warlock

- The Warlock is allowed for players. A Warlock believes the Moon AIs
  await a peaceful humanity. They worship by listening to garbled
  signals and always ask whether the Moon AIs would approve.

- The Warlock's rig is the rare personal receiver and transmitter.

- Rules note: this world leaves the fifth-edition rules, so the class
  becomes an occupation and a set of skills. The flavor stays.

## Followers and the law

- Followers are seen as unlawful trespassers with no rights, because
  no corporation sponsors them.

- Security members of a salvage team may try to stop them. Troopers
  or security may arrest them and bring them back for incarceration.

- The fees from the trespass become a debt, and the followers must
  work it off. Many people in labor camps and factories are
  prisoners working off debts. Some are Moon followers, some are
  trash druids and some are just scavengers.

- Murdering followers is seen as dangerous because of the chance of
  revenge, but sometimes combat is necessary.

## Followers and the trash druids

- Trash druids have sometimes teamed up with Moon followers. There
  is an idea to unify the two groups somehow. Not settled.

- The druids do not trust the Moon AIs. They hold that AI was the
  ultimate case of humanity losing touch with nature and living in an
  illusory world.

- The Moon AIs sometimes judge from a message that they need another
  group, and that is how they have contacted the druids. The druids
  hear them out with skepticism, sometimes agree and sometimes
  decline.

- The follower cleanup work and the druid healing work overlap, so a
  joint errand is possible.

## Followers and the salvage corporation

- A corporation has always listened to the public sermons for clues
  about good salvage opportunities. It sold that information to a
  salvage corporation, which gets the permits and hires the permit
  team.

- The followers go into the ruin first. The corporation watched them
  at work, decided there was probably something worth salvaging, and
  then sent the team. The followers are bait without knowing it.

- Tension starts when the salvage team arrives at a site where the
  followers are already working and making progress.

- The supervisor wants the data the followers have gathered, and
  offers to let them leave without being arrested if they share all
  of it. The offer is the supervisor's private bargain. The security
  team may not honor it, so the player must judge whether the
  supervisor can deliver.

- Supervisors are told to make field decisions that maximize the
  chance of success. Negotiation can be best, depending on the size
  and weapons of the other parties.

- Corporations want exclusive control of the ruins and do not want
  other factions to obtain valuable technology.

## The Warlock's starting position

- The Warlock starts with the congregation's cleanup party, heading
  for the ruin. The rig has caught a sermon about a danger there.
  The Warlock is the party's ear.

- The debt hook is an obligation to the congregation, not money. It
  matches the druid's obligation to the elders.

- If the player is a Warlock, the salvage team are NPCs and arrive
  later, as hostile NPCs who want the followers' data.

## Who listens, and what they do (added 2026-11-02)

Full text is in `decisions.md`, section "Who listens to the Moon AIs".
The short version:

- Few guaranteed channels. Citizens must pay for receiver licenses.
  Recordings spread on black-market networks.

- Followers are mostly ordinary people who keep their lives, send
  money to other members and volunteer time. Many hide the belief and
  climb corporate ranks. Better equipment brings more information.

- The "lunatics" are often socially excluded and have a subculture in
  the cities. Corporations mostly see them as weird but harmless.

- City members fund charity houses and outpost monasteries (radio
  stations in the wilderness, sometimes on land bought from a
  corporation). Teams there maintain the equipment and guard it.

- The Moon AIs direct volunteer projects: reclaiming polluted land,
  or fetching technology or information from ruins. Followers often
  buy the salvage rights first, to avoid conflict.

- The Moon AIs see the corporations as a threat, for their obsession
  with profit. They also fear rogue AIs.

- Some followers add hidden signal implants. "Never feel alone ever
  again."

- Hook: an NPC with dual loyalties, a corporate insider who is a
  secret follower on a secret mission.

## Why the hospital party had no rights

- The sermon came too late to buy salvage rights. The danger sounded
  urgent, so the monastery went in on faith. The corporation, which
  hears the same sermons, can call them trespassers and buy the
  rights itself.

## Followers' supporters inside the corporation

- The followers have powerful supporters in the corporation. Someone
  above the supervisor ordered that the followers be negotiated with
  and not treated violently. The supervisor fears for his career if
  he angers a sympathetic superior. Details are in `decisions.md`,
  section "The supervisor's competing goals".
