# future-trash-world: decisions so far

Working name. Matt is co-authoring this world by interview: Claude
asks one question at a time, Matt answers, Claude takes notes. This
file is the record. `bible.md` has not been written yet. Matt asked
to draft it later, after the open questions in `next-questions.md`.

Status: interview paused on 2026-10-25, after question 34.

## The pitch

A post-apocalyptic future, not fantasy. Much more dangerous than a
fantasy world. Vast areas have no rule of law. Technology is a mix of
primitive living and modern tech, salvaged and repaired from the
ruins of the old cities. There was no single cause of the collapse.
Many things went wrong at once.

## Time and shape of the collapse

- The collapse was slow and came in waves over decades. Some regions
  fell long ago and others are still falling.

- Some wealthy regions are still civilized, much like the rich and
  poor today. Many people feel they once had a nicer life.

- The stable places have a fortress mentality, and city-states have
  returned.

## The five collapse threads

1. **Rogue AI.**

   - AIs became uncontrollable once they passed a certain level of
     processing power. Some turned destructive at once. Others
     destroyed themselves.

   - Some left the planet and built a huge robot city on the Moon.
     They study space and look for other intelligent life. It can be
     seen through an ordinary telescope.

   - Even simple, safe AIs could be corrupted remotely by rogue
     ones. People decided the only good robot is a dead robot, and
     technology stopped advancing.

2. **The plastic-eating microbe.**

   - A fungus or bacterium that eats petroleum-based hydrocarbons,
     maybe a mutation of a strain made to clean up oil spills.

   - Plastic rots fast, so manufactured goods do not last. Similar
     microbes destroy crude oil, so oil is scarce and costly because
     it must be stored carefully.

3. **Chemical contamination.** Explains the giant mutant insects,
   in the style of the film *Them!*. The insect origin is left open.

4. **Economic collapse.** Governments merged with economic interests.

5. **Climate shift.** Deserts have spread across most of Ohio. Water
   is scarce except near the Great Lakes and the rivers.

## The AI taboo

- Working assumption: most places ban AI and smart machines, a few
  city-states use sandboxed automation, and the rich quietly rely on
  more than they admit. Matt never confirmed this directly, but he
  confirmed the quiet-reliance version later (see registration).

- Cybernetic parts controlled by the human nervous system are
  acceptable. Autonomous machines are not.

- "Hacking" is the in-world word for any unauthorized work on robot
  AI. It is a path to getting rich quickly, or getting killed.

- Dormant AIs are a kind of treasure. Players can dig up old robots,
  salvage them for parts, or revive them and patch them so they
  cannot be corrupted.

- Penalty for being caught with a working robot: assumed to depend on
  who you are. The poor lose the machine, the wealthy and the
  troopers have approved ones. Not confirmed by Matt.

- Registration: a trooper may ask for proof of registration for
  computer work. Registration means a corporation authorizes you and
  implicitly takes responsibility for what happens.

- Officially there is no AI work. The top tiers quietly run
  locked-down systems.

## The Moon AIs and the signal war

- The Moon AIs are waiting for humanity to live up to its potential
  and are trying to help that happen.

- Some people worship them as spiritual leaders, even as angels.
  Others think the AIs owe humanity, because humans made them, and
  should use their power to help.

- They broadcast sermons and advice, including medical advice, to
  anyone who can send them a message.

- Rogue Earth AIs jam the signals, and intercept and answer with bad
  advice. Nobody can be sure what to trust without powerful
  technology.

- Public sermons are heard on ordinary radios and are easy to
  counterfeit. Personal messages and answers need a rare receiver and
  transmitter. A Warlock's rig is an object worth stealing.

- The Moon AIs do not like to help one faction of humanity hurt
  another, and they ignore greedy requests. A rogue AI may fill the
  silence with a trap.

## Rogue AIs on Earth

- Two kinds: many small scattered programs (in drones, ruins and
  networks), and a few ancient named ones that act as long-term
  villains. Matt chose both, plus dormant ones.

- Rogue AI identities and goals are plot. They stay out of the
  player-safe `bible.md`.

## The map: Cleveland, Ohio

- The playable area is the Cleveland area and its trade routes to
  Akron and the other big cities. Other regions may come later.

- Cleveland's center is ruins, off-limits without a permit.

- Moving outward: the fence line (poorest, people living against the
  walls), then the inner suburbs, then the wealthy outer suburbs.
  Further out means richer. Past the far edge things get worse again
  until the troopers no longer patrol.

- There are no literal city walls. Patrols hold the stable zones.

- The big roads between cities are mostly safe. Land off the trade
  routes is ignored and lawless.

- Akron and Cleveland are friendly. Each is run by its own
  corporations, which form alliances and merge over time into
  massive complex hierarchies.

- Lawless land: dry scrub and dust over half-buried suburbs and
  highways. Some waterways are polluted. New Amish farms look
  beautiful after days in the dust bowl.

- Lake Erie is still fresh water, the lifeline, and water is power.

## Society in the cities

- A caste system like medieval nobles and peasants, but styled as
  employees of giant corporations. Government and economy have
  merged and bureaucrats run everything.

- Your rank in the bureaucracy is your wealth and your social status.
  Some people climb, but the system is becoming nearly hereditary.

- Corporations settle disputes by consensus, sometimes through
  contracts and sometimes through bribes. No court system exists.

- The poorest workers are born in debt. They borrow for education,
  housing and food. The system tests aptitude and decides whether to
  train them. Those not picked up slip through the cracks and live as
  thieves, scavengers or beggars. The model is the poorest parts of
  an Indian city today.

- Plantation-style farms hold workers who labor for food and lodging
  and never get ahead, like serfs.

## Troopers

- Ohio State Troopers, grown into cybernetically enhanced
  "RoboCop" enforcers who are also genetically modified (stronger,
  faster). It is biological hacking.

- Every corporation sponsors its own troopers, or hires troopers
  who answer to the corporation. They are mercenaries hired as
  police, with a bias based on social status.

- Near the cities they keep hostile people off the trade routes, to
  protect cargo and VIPs from theft and ransom. They ignore the
  peasants who live on their own.

- Open: can an AI corrupt a trooper's nervous-system link?

## Factions and beliefs

- **The New Amish** (name provisional). Isolationist communities in
  the wilderness. They use modern weapons for defense, reject the
  sinful city folk, and blame immorality and God's wrath for the
  collapse. They remediate damaged land, grow luxury food for the
  city elites, and some have become wealthy.

- **The lake movement.** A subculture with varying commitment, like
  animal rights today.

  - Radicals live in the wilderness and try to heal the earth.
    They are called "trash druids" because they worship nature and
    rummage through trash, decomposing it to restore the land.

  - Insiders in big corporations say the earth can be healed but
    argue that some pollution now is needed for security.

  - Fatalists think nothing can be done and only want to keep the
    power on to prevent more violence.

- **Foundry Houses** (name provisional). Industrialists who pollute
  the lake for the sake of industry.

- **Moon AI worshippers.** See above.

- **The city bureaucracy** is its own secular faith in rank.

- Working assumption: the belief groups avoid each other, trade when
  they must, and a few people secretly belong to another group.

## Food, power and money

- Good food is a luxury. Corporations manufacture food: fungus,
  farmed insects, hydroponic vegetables and tank-raised fish. The
  wealthy eat free-range meat and sun-grown vegetables. The poor eat
  lab-grown fungus and climate-adapted grains that taste bad.

- Water costs more the further you are from the lake. Assumed. Not
  confirmed by Matt.

- Many currencies. People are paid in the currency of the corporation
  they work for or partly own. Exchange rates change constantly.
  Barter is also common.

- Electricity is available to everyone. Batteries are much better, so
  large amounts of power can be stored. People sometimes pay for
  services in stored electricity.

- As oil ran out and governments collapsed, industries built
  micro-nuclear plants. Legends tell of people who found an abandoned
  one, repaired it, charged batteries and got rich. Other legends
  tell of meltdowns that poisoned the nearby land.

## Treasure hunting

- With a permit from a corporation you may enter the ruins, but the
  corporation takes everything and pays what it calls fair. Without a
  permit you sneak in, risk getting caught, and need a black-market
  buyer.

- Pay is a mix of a base share, adjusted by rank and negotiation.
  Rough placeholder: about a third of value from a permit, about two
  thirds on the black market, minus the middleman's cut and the risk.

- The black market is a pyramid. Middlemen buy from scavengers and
  resell at higher prices, and may have you killed if you go to the
  main buyer. The wealthy deal with the top. The joke: scavengers do
  the hard parts and get the least pay. Assumed shape: a mix of tiers
  and corporate fronts.

- Players may try to sell at the best price. Buyers may not trust
  them, and middlemen who find out may retaliate. It is a choice, not
  a fixed rule.

- Salvage targets: old batteries (drained), solar panels and other
  power gear, micro-nuclear plants, dormant robots.

## Healing

- Slow rest is free. Fast healing costs.

- Forms: healing "potions" (costly, and some sellers pass off fakes
  that make people sick), nanobots (distrusted, because AIs can
  corrupt them), clinics that grow replacement parts and graft them,
  and regular medicine.

- A clinic that heals you sends a bill that may require a task. The
  medicine often needs a special last dose, given after the task.
  Without it the healing reverses or gets worse.

- Assumed: death is permanent, and any return from death carries the
  same kind of heavy leash.

## Rules mapping

- Magic is reskinned as technology. Spell data keeps its structure.

- Tone: between gritty and heroic. Healing and gear are costly,
  characters level up normally, and the danger comes from scarcity.

- Everyone is human. No fantasy monsters. The main danger is people,
  the scariest is rogue machines, the weirdest is biology
  (the giant insects, contamination, mutations).

- Class mapping, confirmed by Matt:

  - Fighter: soldier or trooper type.

  - Rogue: scavenger, thief, infiltrator.

  - Ranger: wasteland scout, guide.

  - Barbarian: raider or feral survivor.

  - Bard: broadcaster, fixer, face.

  - Druid: trash druid, a decomposer who works with rot and fungus.

  - Monk: disciplined augment or gene-athlete.

  - Paladin: zealot (New Amish, lake movement or Moon cult).

  - Sorcerer: gene-hacked mutant.

  - Wizard: **Engineer**, who uses swarms of nanobots or AI drones
    and accepts the risks. Permitted, or a rogue hacker.

  - Cleric: a person trained to use biological super-science to heal
    or harm.

  - Warlock: allowed for players. Believes the Moon AIs await a
    peaceful humanity. Worships by listening to garbled signals and
    always asks whether the Moon AIs would approve.

- Idea for the mechanics stage: a hacker's failed or very powerful
  use risks **corruption**, where the swarm or drone turns on them
  or is hijacked by a rogue AI.

- Idea for the mechanics stage: floating exchange rates are hard for
  a script that owns every number. One stable reference price, with
  each currency a modifier set by region.
