# The relic

## Tone

Heist and dread. It starts as a respectable, slightly shady job from a
kind priest and ends in the dark under a mountain with the dead waking
up behind you. Grim, with relief from the windmill village, a stubborn
goat farmer and a canary that will not stop talking.

## Start place

The great temple of the Lantern Church in Haldmouth, at morning watch, a
few days after the spring ships arrived. The plague has started in the
dock streets, and the line of the sick outside the temple is long.

## The opening scene

An acolyte, Brother Wick, finds the hero and says that a priest wants to
meet them on a grave and private matter. In the temple, Brother Severin
is blessing the sick one by one. When the last one shuffles away, he
thanks the hero for coming, studies their face, and asks for their
discretion before he says another word.

In a tiny library office he shows them old paintings: an angel giving a
lantern to paladins and clerics, and that lantern driving back the dead
and healing the sick. Then a map: the mountain shrine on a peak of the
Fneb mountains, run by a small faith that is not the Lantern Church. Its
relic, he says, is that same lantern. It is not safe there. Bandits
could take it, or worse, the enemies who want the old age of slavery
back.

He wants it swapped, not stolen. From a box carved with runes he lifts a
replica, blessed so that pilgrims will still be healed. Get in through
the old caves, swap the lanterns, harm no one, and tell no one. If the
guards catch them, the church will not know them.

He gives maps to the cave entrance, through the caves and of the
stronghold, the replica, and coin for the road. Wick will ride with them
as far as the border station and wait there.

Start the deadline as a secret counter: `track C --name "conjunction"
--set 50 --secret`.

## What is really going on here

Everything Severin says about the paintings is true. Nothing he says
about his reasons is. The lantern is the seal on the Eye (see the
metaplot). The replica heals, a little, but it seals nothing. Corvane
told Severin to have the lantern taken, because Severin cannot walk into
the shrine himself without being noticed, and because the shrine's
guards would die before they let it go.

Once the real lantern leaves its altar, the necropolis under the shrine
starts to wake. Severin will tell Wick to sail out and drop it in the
middle of the Ghost Sea. Wick, terrified, will sell it to a thieves'
guild instead.

## The local villain

**Brother Severin,** in his first move against the seal. In this door he
is warm, patient and generous: the hero should like him. His motive is
the one in the metaplot. He believes the Yoke would end plague and
hunger, and he is willing to pay any price to get there. His next three
moves, if nobody stops him:

1. He sends Wick to dispose of the lantern, and visits the temple
   library to burn a few of Father Hallam's papers.

2. He rides to the valley with two acolytes and goes down into the
   necropolis. One acolyte comes back a ghoul, and the other flees, mad.

3. He leads the dead up out of the caves and takes the shrine, while the
   guards try to use the replica.

## Other people

- **Brother Wick,** Severin's acolyte. Nervous, honest, and out of his
  depth.

- **Father Hallam,** Severin's mentor, very old and walking with a cane,
  who still blesses the sick every morning. He led the Burnings, and he
  does not like to talk about them.

- **The border station's captain,** who has lost a patrol to ghouls and
  wants someone to believe her.

- **The windmill village innkeeper,** who has nothing awesome to offer,
  but the farmers are friendly.

- **Ansel Rook,** a goat farmer who has trapped a ghoul in his barn and
  comes running to the inn for help in the middle of the night.

- **The canary,** Ansel's talking canary, a Wager avatar (see the
  metaplot). If the hero helps Ansel, the canary announces that it is
  going with them, and Ansel agrees, as long as they bring it back. It
  nudges, teases and never gives an order. It never needs food, but it
  does enjoy treats.

- **Keeper Oda,** head of the shrine's small faith. She is kind and
  suspicious. She knows only that the lantern heals and must never
  leave.

- **The shrine guards,** good people who will not tolerate thieves.

- **Brother Tamas,** a monk from the desert-edge monastery, who arrives
  in Haldmouth later to ask why the shrine has gone silent.

## Companion candidates

- **Brother Wick,** a cleric. Earnest, frightened and loyal to Severin,
  and he can read the old church script. Secret: he already suspects
  that something is wrong with his master, and he is too afraid to say
  so.

- **Nell Harrow,** a rogue. A scout of the border station, sharp, quiet
  and sure of herself, who wants to see the caves her patrol never came
  back from. Secret: she found one of her patrol walking around after he
  died, and she has told no one.

- **Ilse Varga,** a wizard. A scholar of dead scripts from the great
  temple library, who begs to come once she sees the maps. Secret: she
  wants the necropolis's books on raising the dead, and tells herself it
  is only for study.

## If the hero does nothing

The conjunction counter shows the days left.

- **50:** Severin hires the Red Knife footpads instead. They are clumsy
  and kill a shrine guard.

- **45:** The lantern is gone and the replica is on the altar. The
  windmill village loses two goats a night.

- **40:** Wick sells the lantern to a Haldmouth thieves' guild and
  starts drinking himself numb.

- **30:** Severin goes down into the necropolis. Ghouls dig up the
  valley's graves every week.

- **20:** The dead take the shrine. The guards' replica does nothing.

## Fights

- **Ghouls in Ansel's barn:** 2 ghouls, one eating a goat and one in the
  rafters. A farmer the ghouls kill rises as a ghoul unless a cleric
  blesses the body.

- **Ghouls on the road:** if the hero camps outside in the outlands, 2
  ghouls circle the camp and try to surprise it.

- **The cave entrance:** a giant spider waits in a trapdoor pit in the
  mud cave behind the boulders. Each climber makes a Perception check
  (DC 13) to see its eyes and an Athletics check (DC 12) to climb down.
  Fail both and it attacks with surprise. A coin bag lies in the pit
  among the bones.

- **The way out.** Once the lantern leaves the altar, the dead stir
  below. On the way back down, 4 skeletons and 2 ghouls come up the
  necropolis stair. The narrow bridges over the cavern let one fight at
  a time, and a Strength check (DC 13) can send a skeleton off the edge.
  The stream tunnel is a crawl, so only the last person in line can
  fight, with disadvantage. The real lantern, held up, gives every
  undead within 30 feet disadvantage on attacks, but it lights up the
  whole cavern, and more come. A hero who goes out through the shrine's
  front gate instead faces 2 guards, who fight to subdue.

- **Getting caught:** 2 guards and Keeper Oda, who would rather talk.

**Sneaking through the shrine.** It is a group Stealth challenge: three
successes before two failures. The DC depends on the watch: 16 by day,
when pilgrims are about, 14 at first watch, and 12 at second watch, when
the guards are tired. One success can be traded for a clever plan, such
as a distraction, a disguise or a forged pilgrim's token. At two
failures a guard raises the alarm. The swap itself is a Sleight of Hand
check (DC 13). On a failure, the lantern's light flickers as it leaves
the altar, and someone notices.

The prayer robe Severin gives a cleric hero: `item add C --who <id>
--name "prayer robe" --note "Pray for one action: allies within 30 ft
gain 1d4 temporary HP. Once per long rest."`. Track it with `track C
--name "<name> prayer robe" --set 1`.

## Local secrets

- The angels in the temple paintings carry the same light as the oasis
  carvings in the desert (R2).

- The shrine faith's oldest rule is "the light must never leave the
  mountain". Keeper Oda does not know why (R2).

- The arch over the necropolis stair names an immortal mage, Corvane,
  and "the Eye below". The carved map in the cavern shows the old
  fortress built on the rim of a crater (R1).

- Inside the replica's base, scratched small, is a sigil that matches
  the arch's carvings (R4).

- Father Hallam's papers, the ones Severin has not burned yet, record a
  baby carried out of a burning grove (R4).

- The canary refuses to say whether the job is right. It only asks why
  Severin did not come himself (R5, for later doors).

## The way in to the larger mystery

- **R1:** the necropolis arch and the crater map show that the dead walk
  because of what is buried below.

- **R2:** the paintings, the shrine's rule and the lantern's effect on
  the dead show that the lantern is a seal.

- **R4:** the sigil in the replica and Hallam's papers lead back to
  Severin, and to whose son he is.

## Ways it can end

- **The swap succeeds** and the hero is paid. The dead begin to rise,
  and the hero has to set it right: get the lantern back from the
  thieves' guild, or go down into the necropolis to stop Severin.

- **The hero refuses the swap,** or returns the lantern and tells Keeper
  Oda the truth. The shrine holds, and Severin needs a new plan and a
  new servant.

- **The hero keeps the real lantern.** It is a powerful weapon against
  the dead, and it is no longer sealing anything.

- **The hero is caught.** The church disowns them, and the shrine's
  cells are cold, but Keeper Oda listens.

- **The hero dies in the dark** under the mountain, and the dead keep
  walking.
