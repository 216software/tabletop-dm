# The oasis

## Tone

Plague-city dread, then a long, hot, strange journey. Grim, with relief
from the people met on the road: a tavern family, a stubborn old widow,
kind and nervous monks, and a witch who is not what anyone expects. The
desert should feel like a puzzle that is alive.

## Start place

Haldmouth, a month after the spring ships came in. Black flags hang over
the dock streets. The temple courtyards are full of the sick and their
families.

## The opening scene

The hero is in a Haldmouth street market at morning watch. Masked
footpads knock old Hob, a produce seller the hero knows by sight, off
his ox cart and start going through it. Hob yells for help. If the hero
steps in, the footpads fight briefly and run. Then Hob says they are
Mother Sall's people, and that Sall's lot paint their blades with plague
blood. The hero should go to the great temple for a blessing at once.

Anyone the footpads cut makes no roll yet. A few days later, the DM
rolls a DC 12 Constitution save for each. A failure means black marks
under the fingernails: the ship plague. Start a counter for each one:
`track C --name "<name> plague days" --set 30`.

(This hook is based on an idea by Boojamon on Reddit.)

At the temple, Sister Amity offers the blessing and a bargain. She has
found a ritual in the library that cured the last plague, eighty years
ago. It needs water from an oasis in the cursed desert. Will the hero
fetch it?

Start the deadline as a secret counter: `track C --name "conjunction"
--set 55 --secret`.

## What is really going on here

The ship plague is natural. The cure is real. The oasis water heals
because it runs past the crystal that seals the Thirst, the Old One
fragment under the desert (see the metaplot). Filling flasks does the
seal no harm. Breaking or moving the crystal would wake the Thirst, and
the desert would start to spread.

Mother Sall wants the cure to fail. She survived the plague, and it has
made her rich: she sells false cures, protection and graves. The cabal
has been visiting her dreams for months, and she does not know what they
are.

Hessa the witch is the one person in the desert who understands the
seal. She has kept it from harm for twenty years without quite knowing
why.

## The local villain

**Mother Sall,** boss of the Red Knife footpads in Haldmouth. She is a
big, cheerful woman with plague scars, who calls everyone "duck". Her
motive is money and the power she has never had before: the plague has
made her the most important person in the dock streets. Her next three
moves, if nobody stops her:

1. She sends footpads to follow the hero and learn the route.

2. She pays the highwaymen on the lowland road to stop the hero on the
   way home.

3. She sends a rider to the monastery with a forged letter that cancels
   the abbot's help.

## Other people

- **Sister Amity,** a cleric of the great temple. She is a short-sighted
  scholar, brave in a quiet way, and tired. She gives the hero a sealed
  letter for the abbot and another for the dowager.

- **Brother Severin,** seen once at the temple, kneeling with the sick,
  gentle and tireless. He tells the hero to hurry. Let the hero like
  him.

- **Old Hob,** a produce seller who drives an ox cart into the city
  every morning. He owes the hero, and he hears everything.

- **The Tullys,** the large family who run the Lowland River House, won
  in a card game by their great-grandparents. They serve fish stew and
  rumours. Use the rumour table in the bible.

- **Lady Wenna Brannagh,** the dowager, the last of her line, who runs
  night militia patrols against bandits from the barrens. She feeds the
  hero, stables the horses and grumbles about the church.

- **Abbot Cuthwin and the monks,** the star watchers at the edge of the
  barrens. They give the hero the atlas of the desert. Each map in it is
  true only at certain watches, marked with a sundial or a star chart.
  Their real fear is a star alignment they have started calling the Long
  Conjunction.

- **Hessa, the witch,** in her mud house on a hill, with a buzzard
  familiar, giant scorpions that treat her as their mother, and castaway
  children she is teaching. She may heal the hero, curse them or hold a
  séance, depending on how she is treated.

- **The beggar in the desert,** who asks for food and drink at the
  hero's camp. Kindness earns a blessing, a shrug earns a warning, and
  cruelty earns a curse that gives strength and slowly takes the mind.
  He is one of the Wager's avatars (see the metaplot), and he is testing
  the hero.

## Companion candidates

- **Jory Tull,** a rogue. The youngest son of the Lowland River House
  family, a card sharp and a charmer who wants to see the world. Secret:
  he owes the Red Knife money, and Mother Sall has told him to report
  the hero's route.

- **Brother Pell,** a cleric. Sister Amity's young assistant, earnest
  and well read, who knows the ritual by heart. Secret: his fingernails
  have started to turn black, and he hides it in gloves.

- **Sergeant Edda Thorne,** a fighter. Lady Wenna's militia sergeant,
  scarred, dry and fearless, who joins at the estate. Secret: she was in
  the mercenary company that burned a grove in the Burnings, and she
  still dreams of it.

## If the hero does nothing

The conjunction counter shows the days left.

- **55:** The plague spreads to the lowlands. The baron flees to his
  country house.

- **50:** Sall's people burn a rival's warehouse, and the city watch
  stops patrolling the docks.

- **45:** Sister Amity sends someone else, who never comes back.

- **40:** Brother Pell dies, or is very sick.

- **35:** Bandits from the barrens raid the dowager's estate.

- **30:** The temple courtyards are full of the dead. Sall controls the
  dock streets.

Meanwhile, on the metaplot's clock, the lantern goes missing and the
dead grow restless near the mountains.

## Fights

- **Footpads in the market:** 3 bandits, who flee at the first real
  wound.

- **The highwaymen at the crossroads:** a bandit captain and 4 bandits,
  two of them on a wagon with crossbows and two more hidden in the trees
  (Perception DC 12 to spot). They are all infected. One of them spits
  bloody phlegm in the hero's face (Constitution save DC 12, or catch
  the plague). This idea is by MelancholyOcelot on Reddit.

- **Ghouls in the mass graves,** if the hero goes near the burning pits
  at night: 2 ghouls.

- **Desert raiders,** from the Bone Bead tribe: they throw poisoned
  darts and run when hurt, then come back with more.

- **A young giant scorpion,** at night, after the horses. A full-grown
  one is far too much for a hero below level 3: let it be heard and
  avoided.

**Crossing the desert.** Each watch of travel is one Survival check, DC
13: +2 at morning and afternoon, +0 at dawn and dusk, and -2 at night. A
success adds 1 to the `track C --name "desert progress"` counter. A
failure means a second check: a success on that is a wasted watch, and a
failure takes 1 away. The oasis is at 6, and so is the way back. The
right map from the atlas gives advantage, but only in its own watch.
Each watch also brings one desert event: a buzzard watching, a ruined
caravan (a thieves' cant notebook inside gives +2 if someone can read
it), an abandoned camp with human bones in the fire, thorny plants that
stand in for a day's water, a cave to camp in, a slave caravan passing,
the beggar, or, after a few wasted watches, Hessa's hill.

## Local secrets

- The oasis carvings show angels with a lamp of light above people
  sharing water. They match the lantern paintings in the great temple
  (R2).

- Behind the waterfall, a seam of crystal glows when the sun is
  overhead. Sister Amity's ritual notes speak of "the light beneath the
  water" (R2).

- Mother Sall dreams of a smiling stranger who thanks her. So does the
  slave caravan's captain (R3).

- Hessa's séance shows, in no order: a lamp with a glowing crystal
  inside, tunnel walls carved with runes, a burning temple and an angry
  mob, a black scythe on an orange flag, and an army of the dead.

- Hessa lost a baby son when her grove burned, thirty years ago. She
  will say so to someone she trusts (R4).

## The way in to the larger mystery

- **R2:** the oasis carvings, the crystal behind the water and the
  ritual notes show that the oasis is a seal site, and that the lantern
  is the same kind of thing.

- **R3:** Sall's dreams and the slave captain's dreams are the cabal's
  touch.

- **R4:** Hessa's lost son. If the hero asks about the year of the
  Burnings in the temple library, Hallam's papers show a baby carried
  out of a grove that year. A hero who puts that together with Severin's
  age has the truth.

## Ways it can end

- **The cure arrives in time.** The ritual works, the plague breaks, and
  Sister Amity owes the hero. Sall loses her grip on the docks, or makes
  a bargain.

- **The cure arrives late.** It still works, but the city has buried too
  many, and Sall rules the dock streets.

- **The hero carries word to Hessa, or from her.** Her pact can end.
  That thread runs into the relic and valley-farm doors.

- **The hero breaks the seal** for the crystal's power, or to sell it.
  The desert begins to grow, by a mile a season.

- **The hero dies of the plague** on the way home, unless a companion
  carries the flasks the rest of the way.
