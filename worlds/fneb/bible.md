# Fneb: world bible

Everything in this file is common knowledge. A person who grew up under
the Fneb mountains knows it, or has heard it said. The DM may tell any
of it to a player. The hidden truth of the world is in a different file.

## Fneb in one paragraph

Fneb is the country where three lands meet: the cold Ghost Sea to the
north, the cursed desert to the east, and the Fneb mountains to the
south, with a fertile river valley caught between them. The old kingdom
here broke into baronies long ago, and the Baron of Haldmouth, who holds
the port at the river's mouth, is the strongest of them. The Lantern
Church owns most of the farmland and takes its rent in crops. Merchant
guilds hire rangers to keep the mountain trade road open. Everyone knows
the old story of how mortals were once slaves, and how they freed
themselves by simply refusing, all at once. Everyone also burns their
dead, and nobody likes to say exactly why.

## Tone

- **The base note is grim.** Plague years, hungry winters, bandits on
  the road, slavers in the desert, and the dead that do not always stay
  down. Life is hard and often unfair, and most people are just getting
  by.

- **The relief is human, and often funny.** A tavern won in a card game,
  turnips nobody admits to eating, a constable older than his own war
  hammer, talking birds with opinions. The DM uses it to let the player
  breathe, never to undercut a real loss.

- **People are better than their rulers.** Rents rise, priests preach
  purity, barons flee their cities. Ordinary people still share bread,
  hide fugitives and leave gifts out for the elves.

- **Fear is the real weapon.** The worst things in Fneb work because
  people turn on each other. A hero who makes people trust each other
  again is doing something powerful.

## What everyone knows about the past

This is the story every child hears. The details change with the teller.

- **Before.** There was the Green Mother (the earth under our feet), the
  Sun who warms her, the forests, the beasts and us. Some say the first
  people grew from fruit in the deep forest, and some say they hatched
  from the stones of a falling star. People remember this age fondly,
  and joke that we spent it fighting each other over pride.

- **The sorcerers.** We grew fat and greedy, and the greediest became
  sorcerers, who used their power to rule each other. Many tellers say
  this is why what came next came.

- **The Fall.** The Old Ones came down in a storm of falling stars. The
  land was scarred with craters, the sky filled with dust, the weather
  broke and never fully mended. Something foul rose out of the craters.
  The Old Ones were huge and strong and slow, and the air of this world
  tired them, and lightning hurt them.

- **The Young Ones.** So the Old Ones made servants who could live here:
  smaller, quicker, and in the shapes we now call angels and demons. The
  Young Ones drove mortals into the mines to dig what the Old Ones
  craved.

- **The Yoke.** Our sorcerers fought and lost, because the Young Ones
  turned some of us against the rest. In the end, mortals ran the whole
  system of their own slavery: overseers, tithe-takers, informers, all
  mortal.

- **The Refusal.** One day, everyone stopped. Nobody gave an order, and
  nobody obeyed one. Seeing that, some of the Young Ones changed sides.
  The Sun struck the Old Ones with fire from the sky, the Green Mother
  split the ground open, and the Old Ones were broken and buried deep.
  The Young Ones who had joined us faded away soon after.

- **Since then.** Kingdoms rose and fell. The old kingdom built the
  roads and the border stones, then broke into baronies. The last great
  plague was eighty years ago. A generation ago, hardline priests of the
  Lantern Church led the Burnings, when druid groves in the valley were
  put to the torch. People do not agree about whether that was right,
  and they do not like to argue about it in public.

What the story means is argued about in every tavern. To the Lantern
Church it is a story about faith and the Sun's gift. To farmers it is
about refusing to be squeezed. To barons it is a story best not told too
often.

## Telling time

Nobody in Fneb counts hours. The day has six watches of about four hours
each:

| Watch | Roughly | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Dawn | 4 to 8 in the morning | Its middle is the moment of dawn |
| Morning | 8 to noon | The easiest time to find your way |
| Afternoon | noon to 4 | Easy going, and hot in the desert |
| Dusk | 4 to 8 in the evening | Its middle is the moment of dusk |
| First watch | 8 to midnight | Hard to find your way |
| Second watch | midnight to 4 | Hardest. When the dead walk, if ever |

On a good road in good weather, a walker covers about 25 miles in a day
of eight hours, and a rider about 60. A horse can gallop only in short
bursts.

## The places

### Haldmouth (the capital)

A walled port where the Hald runs into the Ghost Sea. The sea freezes in
winter, and the spring festival marks the day the ice breaks and the
first merchant ships come in. It has busy docks, crowded street markets,
guildhalls, and the great old temple of the Lantern Church with its
library. Under the streets there are sewers and older catacombs that
nobody maps.

### The Ghost Sea

Cold, grey and fog-bound. Sailors call it the Ghost Sea for the lights
they see on it at night, and say a ship that follows the lights does not
come home.

### The lowlands

Just outside the city walls, every scrap of land is in use: cottages,
workshops, tiny farm plots, canals and little bridges everywhere. A
day's ride out is the **Lowland River House**, a big old aristocrats'
house by a wooden bridge, now an inn run by a large peasant family whose
great-grandparents won it in a card game. It serves fish stew, and
everyone ends up there to trade stories.

### The hill country

Gravel roads, sheep on the hills, more forest and pasture and fewer
plots. Great estates sit in the sides of the hills. The best known is
the **dowager's estate**, a walled house whose family has kept order out
here for generations. The last of the line is an old widow who still
sends her militia out on night patrols.

### The border and the ranger station

A stone column the height of a man on a horse marks the edge of the
barony. Inside it, people owe homage and get the baron's law. Outside
it, they get neither. Near the column is a **ranger station**, paid for
by the merchant guilds, where rangers set out to patrol the trade road
and root out bandits.

### The barrens

Past the border: dry, steep, thorny hills, dead-looking trees, buzzards
and goatherds. Outlaws live here because the baron's men will not
follow. The **monastery** stands on a hill at the barrens' edge, a
walled tower full of telescopes, where monks watch the stars and the
desert and write everything down. They are kind and wary, because
bandits have raided them before.

### The cursed desert

East of the barrens, a desert of canyons and sand. People say its paths
move with the sun and the stars, and that a map of it is true only at
certain hours. Slave caravans cross it from the Eastern Kingdom. Desert
tribes live there and are feared. Somewhere in it is an **oasis** with
blue water and carvings of people sharing water while angels watch. And
people say a witch lives in a mud house on a hill, with a buzzard for
company.

### The Fneb valley

The fertile valley under the mountains: barley, elf grain and black
turnips, all on Lantern Church land. Farm families pay their rent in
crops at the market scales in the valley town, where buyers bid by the
bushel and the tax collector takes his share. Old families still leave
mead out in their fields for the wood elves. Further up is the **stone
valley** and its windmill village, with an inn where farmers drink, and
the trade road climbing into the passes.

### The Fneb mountains

High, cold and full of caves. The trade road crosses them, and so do
bandits. On one of the tallest peaks stands the **mountain shrine**, an
ancient fortress that now belongs to a small faith that is not the
Lantern Church. Pilgrims climb to it and come back saying they saw a
relic of great holy power and were healed. Local people say the caves
under the mountains go down forever, and that something sleeps at the
bottom.

## The peoples

- **Humans** are most people in the barony.

- **Wood elves** live in the forests at the valley's edge. They are seen
  rarely, mostly at night, and they take the mead the farmers leave out.
  Church law forbids dealing with them. Many farmers quietly do anyway.

- **Half-elves** are rumoured more than met. Every valley has an old
  woman everyone says is "part elf".

- **The desert tribes** are called savages and cannibals in the barony.
  They fight with poisoned darts and arrows. Some say they bring their
  sick children to the witch.

- **The Eastern Kingdom** lies beyond the desert. Its slave caravans are
  huge, guarded by riders with curved swords, and its spies are said to
  be everywhere.

- **Below.** Some say there are whole peoples who live underground and
  never see the Sun. The druids are said to visit them in winter.

A hero may be of any people. It changes the story, not the numbers.

## Faith

- **The Lantern Church** is the great faith of the barony. It keeps the
  story of the Refusal as holy history: the Sun's light, carried in a
  holy lantern given by the angels, drove back the dead, healed the sick
  and founded Haldmouth. Its priests heal and bless, its scholars keep
  the library, and its music fills the great temple. It also owns most
  of the valley's farmland. It forbids any dealing with the elves and
  has always distrusted druids. It is not of one mind: some priests are
  gentle scholars, and some led the Burnings.

- **The Green Mother** is the oldest faith, older than the church, kept
  by farmers in small ways: gifts in the fields, a word said over the
  first furrow. The church tolerates it as long as nobody calls it a
  church.

- **The mountain shrine** belongs to a small, quiet faith of its own.
  The Lantern Church calls its people foolish idolaters, but harmless.

- **The Sun, the Moon and the stars** are family to the Green Mother.
  Who is parent and who is child depends on who circles whom, and on the
  season: the Sun is a cruel father in the summer heat and a loving
  mother in the winter.

## Druids

Druids are real, and most people have met one or know someone who has.

- **They tend forests the way farmers tend fields.** An old grove, cared
  for over centuries, grows in patterns that make their rituals
  stronger.

- **They fight with sticks.** The Way of the Stick is meant to disarm
  and drive off, not to kill. The Way of Mercy uses darts, blowguns and
  sleeping potions, mostly to ease a dying animal out of the world.

- **They dislike metal, but it does not burn them.** A sword means a
  mine and a forest burned for charcoal. They prefer stone and wood.

- **Summer and winter.** In summer they revel and worship the Sun. In
  winter they climb into the mountains and go deep into the caves to
  study, meditate and watch the stars.

- **Moon dances.** On bright nights they dance in the fields with their
  prayer sticks, and the moonlight they gather blesses the crops. This
  is why farmers leave gifts out when the moon is big.

- **Iron and lightning.** A tale says one druid came home from a far war
  carrying an iron rod, and showed the others how it calls down
  lightning. The strongest druids are said to send their minds out into
  the clouds, and you can sometimes see a face in a thunderhead.

- **Night druids.** A cautionary tale, and not everyone believes it.
  When a druid's forest is destroyed, the druid may become a night
  druid: a druid of rot, plague and swarms, who destroys whatever killed
  the forest. The tale everyone knows is of Vessary, a rich city on the
  coast that cut down a sacred forest and then died of the ashmouth, a
  sickness that makes its victims cough up grey ash. Vessary is still
  there, nearly empty. The signs that a night druid has chosen a town
  are rashes, strange insects, sick livestock, failing crops and angry
  weather.

## Power, law and money

- **The Baron of Haldmouth** rules the city and the lowlands, and claims
  everything inside the border stones. Other barons rule the lands
  beyond, and would happily take his if he looked weak.

- **The Lantern Church** is the great landlord. Valley tenants pay rent
  in crops, and how much is set by the local priest. A good season means
  a fair winter, and a bad one means hunger.

- **The merchant guilds** keep the trade road open and pay the rangers.

- **The thieves' guilds** of Haldmouth run protection on the street
  vendors, and fight each other more than the watch.

- **Mercenary companies** will fight for anyone who pays, or who
  promises a share of the spoils.

- **Village law** is a constable or two, usually old and tired.

- **Coin:** gold, silver and copper. Farmers see little gold.

## Magic

- **Sorcery has a bad name.** Everyone knows the sorcerers' greed came
  before the Fall. Wizards are useful and respected, but watched.

- **Sun magic is strong.** It can be stored in crystals dug from deep
  caves, and the church's holiest relics are said to hold it.

- **Moon magic is subtle.** It is good for growing, healing and
  blessing, and poor for fighting.

- **Lightning** is the one thing everyone agrees the Old Ones feared.

- **Curses are real.** People believe that the way you treat a stranger
  can come back on you, and every village has a story of a beggar who
  was more than a beggar.

## The dead

**Everyone in Fneb burns their dead.** The old saying is "burn them, or
they come back". In the valley under the mountains it is not just a
saying: there have always been stories of graves dug up by night and of
the dead walking. Burying a body is an insult, a punishment, or a very
bad sign.

## Monsters and the wild

- **Ghouls and the restless dead** in the valley and the mountain caves.

- **Bandits** everywhere outside the walls, from footpads to whole
  companies in the barrens.

- **Giant scorpions** in the desert, which go for the horses first.

- **Giant spiders** in the mountain caves.

- **Wolves, bears and worse** in the high country.

- **Rats**, bold and many, in Haldmouth's sewers.

## Things people say (rumour, not fact)

The DM may hand these out freely. Some are true, some are not, and some
are half true.

1. The plague never takes thieves and swindlers: the low sort always
   survive it.

2. No traveller crosses the cursed desert without spilling blood. That
   is the curse.

3. The baron would let the plague clear out the poor if he could.

4. Ghouls eat the bodies in the mass graves when the burning pits are
   full.

5. Some noble houses deal with the warlords of the barrens.

6. The Eastern Kingdom has spies in every inn.

7. There are rat-people and mole-people in the catacombs under
   Haldmouth.

8. Eating black turnips makes you stupid. (Or strong. Accounts differ.)

9. Something huge sleeps under the Fneb mountains, frozen, and one day
   it will wake.

10. Talking animals turn up now and then, with advice. Never trust one
    that asks for a treat. Or always give it one. Accounts differ.

## Names

- **Barony people:** plain old names and nicknames. Moe, Durney, Hale,
  Brannagh, Wenna, Tobe, Sall. Old soldiers get names from their scars
  ("Scarback").

- **Church people:** Brother or Sister and a saint's name: Brother
  Aldous, Sister Mercy.

- **Wood elves:** soft names they rarely give to outsiders.

- **The East:** long names with titles, given in a trade tongue the
  barony calls cant.

- **Places:** what they are, said plainly: the Lowland River House, the
  border stone, the windmill village.

## What the DM must keep consistent

- The dead are burned. A buried body is always worth noticing.

- The church owns the valley land, and rent is paid in crops.

- Druids do not use metal weapons by choice, and do not kill when a
  stick will do.

- The six watches are how people say the time. Nobody says "three
  o'clock".

- The desert's maps are true only at certain hours.

- The Refusal is everyone's story. Who tells it, and how, says a lot
  about the teller.

- People are decent and frightened. Fear, not monsters, is what usually
  makes things go wrong.
